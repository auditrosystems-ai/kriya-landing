⚡ Case study, outside the industry it was born in

Three pipelines. Two brands.
One system. Live in under a week.

A pump, motor and gearbox distributor in Kolkata, trading since 1983, running two brands under one roof. Almost nothing about their business resembles mine, which is exactly why they were worth building for.

Sale one

Trade and retail

Enquiry, quotation, negotiation, order, delivery. The ordinary path, and the only one most CRMs model.

Sale two

Government and railways

A technical evaluation and approval gate sits before you are permitted to quote at all. Quoting early is wasted work.

Sale three

Service contracts

Proposed, quote sent, contract active, due for renewal, renewed, lapsed. A renewal lifecycle, not a sale.

A generic CRM hands all three of those the same Lead to Won list. That would have been wrong for two of their three businesses.

What was actually built for them

  • Three separate pipelines, each contact only ever seeing the stages that belong to its own kind of sale
  • Seven customer categories reflecting how they really segment, including dealers, contractors, vendors and institutional buyers
  • Two full quotation templates, one per brand, with two letterheads and two GST numbers, switchable per quotation
  • Their existing CRM data migrated across, with notes, enquiry history and original dates preserved
  • Their entire product range preloaded, with photographs and links to their own website attached to each item
  • Role based visibility, so their highest value accounts stay invisible to the sales login
  • Automatic new enquiry email alerts, routed to whichever staff member the lead belongs to
Why this page matters more than the rest of the brochure. Until this build, the fair criticism of Kriya was that it had only ever run in the business it was designed for. It now runs daily in a second one, in an unrelated trade, with a materially harder workflow than my own.
